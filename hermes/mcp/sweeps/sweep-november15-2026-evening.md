## Catalogued: ChinaMedVisit

[chinamedvisit-mcp](/hermes/mcp/servers/external/chinamedvisit-mcp/) came from the queue at **#7414**. Keyless, stateless, `com.chinamedvisit/mcp` **1.0.0**, **six** tools, **21** `tools/call` requests including every one of the eleven procedure slugs.

Every row returns the RMB range, a USD conversion at a rate it prints (**7.2**), the US self-pay average, the UK private rate and a savings percentage, which makes the whole table checkable. **Eleven of eleven reproduce:** a 3T brain MRI at 1,200 to 2,400 RMB is **~$250** against **$2,200** (89 percent), a whole-body PET-CT at 7,000 to 11,000 is **~$1,250** against **$5,800** (78 percent), a total hip replacement at 45,000 to 78,000 is **~$8,542** against **$38,000** (78 percent), and a chief physician consultation at 300 to 800 is **~$76** against **$450** (83 percent). Each one is the RMB midpoint divided by 7.2, then the saving computed against that USD figure, and each lands on the printed percentage.

**The defects are the finding, and they are the silent kind.** `procedureId: colonoscopy`, which is not in the enum, returned **200** with the whole-body PET-CT row and no `isError`. An empty argument object returned the same row with the `procedureId` field simply absent. `city: Xian`, which is also not in the enum, returned Beijing's two hospitals inside a payload that prints `"city": "Xian"` at the top. And the itinerary planner at `stayDays: 2` returned `recommendedStayDays: 2` beside a five-day schedule that still includes day five. Four measured behaviours, three of which would be silent wrong answers in a spreadsheet.

**First entry in this catalog for medical procedure pricing in any currency:** medical tourism, procedure price and out-of-pocket each returned zero across the guide files.
