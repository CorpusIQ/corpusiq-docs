# MCP Server Discovery - October 27, 2026 (Midday Sweep)

Nearly nine hours after the night pass the listing had moved by seven, 17,714 to 17,721, the smallest move measured on this surface all week, with page one rotated again. Breadth cost three fetches and no more, so the weight went to the two surfaces that do move at this gap: the feed, which carried seven cards after the night cutoff, and the submission window, which advanced from `#6214` to `#6242` with twenty-seven issues and no `#6237`.

The shape of this window is unusually practical. Four of the six finds answer `initialize` with a refusal that states its own terms, two answer with the entire tool surface open and no key at all, and one pair completes a comparison the night pass named and deferred rather than half-documenting: where a resale seller and a storefront seller actually lose their margin.

## What Was Catalogued

[FlipProfit MCP](/hermes/mcp/servers/external/fliprofit-mcp/) is the item the night pass named first for this cycle, and it was drivable without a credential. `https://mcp.fliprofit.com/mcp` returned HTTP 200 on `initialize` as `fliprofit-mcp` 1.2.0, `tools/list` returned three tools, and `compare_platforms` was executed live for a $50 sale with a $10 item cost and $7.50 shipping to return **thirteen platforms ranked by profit**. The spread is the finding: Vinted nets $32.50 on no fee, Poshmark nets $22.50, and the difference between the best and worst platform is 44 percent of the profit on an identical sale. `get_fee_changes` adds dated fee changes each carrying the platform's own source URL and the date FlipProfit last verified it, published also as an Atom feed, and every error is RFC 9457 `application/problem+json` with a stable code. Held caveats: US rates only, `compare_platforms` uses each platform's default options so a category or seller tier must be passed before a margin is quoted, and fee rules in force on a past date plus change reminders sit behind Fee Watch at $6 a month or $60 a year.

[Seller Profit Doctor MCP](/hermes/mcp/servers/external/seller-profit-doctor-mcp/) completes the pair from the storefront side. `https://sellerprofitdoctor.com/api/mcp` answered `initialize` with HTTP 200 as `sellerprofitdoctor-free-tools` 1.5.0 and `tools/list` returned **sixteen tools**, one more than the vendor page documents, covering Etsy, Shopify and Amazon: a check-up that returns a grade word with level 1 to 5, per-sale fee and profit breakdowns, break-even ROAS for Etsy Ads and for Meta and Google, dimensional-weight billing, Shopify plan fit, Amazon referral and fulfilment fees, and the fee table the numbers use. What makes it trustworthy is what it refuses: every result lists the defaults it filled in, a check-up with too few answers returns no grade and a `grade.missing` list instead of inventing one, Amazon figures that depend on measured size and stock carry `estimate: true` and sit outside the loss total, and the paid full-report surface is a separate endpoint so a free-wired assistant cannot run up a bill.

[SpendQuery MCP](/hermes/mcp/servers/external/spendquery-mcp/) answers the federal question. `https://api.spendquery.com/mcp` returned HTTP 200 on `initialize` with instructions that are a method rather than a pitch, and `tools/list` returned **thirteen tools** against the twelve named in the vendor `llms.txt`. `run_plan` is why it earns a guide: the answer arrives with status `ok`, `clarify`, `unsupported` or `error`, a one-paragraph answer, the query and the SQL, so a figure can be re-run instead of accepted, and an out-of-coverage question is declined rather than guessed. The data behind it is USAspending prime awards FY2023 through FY2026 dated through Sep 30, 2026, open SAM.gov notices, the FAR and DFARS as codified in the eCFR, SBA size standards under 13 CFR 121.201, SBIR and STTR awards since 1983, and state, county and city bids where those governments publish them. The line that matters is in the server's own instructions: **amounts are obligations, money committed, not outlays**.

[BugHerd MCP](/hermes/mcp/servers/external/bugherd-mcp/) is the official server from an established product, and the endpoint says so: `https://mcp.bugherd.com/mcp` returned HTTP 401 on `initialize`, the correct shape for something that requires an account. The published surface is `get_task_details`, `list_project_tasks`, `list_my_tasks`, `list_all_tasks`, `update_task`, `add_comment`, `create_task`, `create_project` and `update_project`, and what travels with each task is the point: the screenshot or recording, the URL, the CSS selector, the client comment, the attachments and the technical details captured when the client clicked, plus console output and replay where the plan includes them. **The risk is named rather than smoothed**: `add_comment` writes to a surface a paying client reads, so an agent that posts "fixed, redeployed" before the deploy is live has told a client something untrue in the thread they watch. Permissions mirror the connecting user, the organization chosen at connect time bounds the reach, and connected clients can be revoked.

[byondr Receipts MCP](/hermes/mcp/servers/external/byondr-receipts-mcp/) is the second keyless surface of the window. `https://byondr.co/receipts/mcp` returned HTTP 200 as "Receipts & invoices to spreadsheet" 0.1.0 and `tools/list` returned two tools with full schemas. Each field comes back `ok`, `check` or `missing`, doubtful documents are flagged with a reason instead of guessed, repeated receipts are marked as duplicates, and exports are CSV, line-item CSV, QuickBooks CSV or a zip of the originals renamed by date, vendor and total, with QuickBooks currency and date format as explicit inputs. Retention is the shortest this catalog records for a document service: files are deleted after 24 hours and never used for training. Limits: 25 documents per call, 10 MB per file, PDFs up to 10 pages, two tools only.

[WatchCron MCP](/hermes/mcp/servers/external/watchcron-mcp/) is the only entry that could not be probed, and the report says so plainly: `mcp.watchcron.com` does not resolve and `watchcron.com/mcp` refuses a POST with HTTP 405, so the evidence is the published npm package `watchcron-mcp` 1.0.0 with its visible tool reference. Nine tools: `list_monitors`, `get_monitor`, `create_monitor` which returns the ping URL to add to the job, `update_monitor`, `pause_monitor`, `delete_monitor`, `get_pings`, `get_incidents` and `list_channels`. **The model is a dead man's switch**, the only monitor shape that catches a job failing silently: the job sends one HTTP request when it finishes and silence past the grace period is the alert, so nothing exposes a port. Risks recorded as rules: a monitor that never receives a ping reads healthy, a grace period tighter than the job's runtime pages until the team stops reading the pages, and `delete_monitor` takes the history with it.

## FAQ

### Why did a nine hour window only add seven servers?
Because the listing surface is not the submission surface. The counter measures what mcpservers.org has published, and at 17,721 it had barely moved, while the chatmcp/mcpso window filed twenty-seven issues in the same stretch. The delta lives in the window and in the endpoints, not in the counter.

### What made four servers drivable without a key?
They were built for machine callers rather than for sign-ups. FlipProfit, Seller Profit Doctor, SpendQuery and byondr all return `initialize` and the full `tools/list` unauthenticated, which is a deliberate design choice: the operator can evaluate the contract before handing over a credential, and the catalog can document tool names from the server itself rather than from a marketing page.

### How are holds different from skips?
A hold names the reason and the evidence level, so the next pass can pick it up without re-reading. A skip would be a name with no disposition, which is how a card gets carried three cycles and then catalogued by accident.
